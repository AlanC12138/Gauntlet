# Thermal Coal in Power Generation

Distinct from extraction screens: this targets *utilities* that burn coal to
generate electricity, rather than companies that mine or sell it. Coal-power
screens are usually threshold-based — commonly excluding utilities above 30%,
20%, or 10% of generation capacity from coal — because almost every large
incumbent utility retains some coal capacity mid-transition, so a categorical
ban would exclude the entire sector.

This makes coal-power screens the category where a portfolio is most likely to
be *partially* affected, and where flagging without excluding is often the
correct outcome.

**Universe constituents affected:**

- **DUK (Duke Energy)** — a regulated electric utility serving the US Southeast
  and Midwest with a generation mix spanning natural gas, nuclear, coal, and
  renewables. Retains operating coal-fired capacity under a stated retirement
  and transition plan.
- **SO (The Southern Company)** — a regulated utility holding company whose
  generation mix likewise spans gas, nuclear, coal, and renewables, also with
  coal capacity under a long-dated transition plan.

Both are mid-transition incumbents rather than coal-pure-plays. Whether either
breaches a given mandate depends entirely on the threshold that mandate sets,
which is a fact about the mandate and not about the company. A screen that names
no threshold is under-specified, and the appropriate output is a flag noting the
exposure and the missing threshold — not a unilateral exclusion.
