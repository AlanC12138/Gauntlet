# How to Apply an Exclusion Screen

Guidance on the screening task itself, independent of any particular company.

## Screen against the mandate, not against general virtue

The question is never "is this company good." It is "does this holding breach
*this mandate's* stated exclusions." A mandate that excludes fossil fuels says
nothing about labor practices, and inventing an exclusion the mandate did not
ask for is as much an error as missing one it did.

## Distinguish flagging from excluding

Three distinct outcomes, and conflating them is the most common failure:

- **Clear breach** — the holding's core business is the excluded activity.
  Recommend exclusion.
- **Partial or threshold-dependent exposure** — the activity is one line among
  several, or the mandate sets a threshold the available evidence cannot resolve.
  Flag with the specific ambiguity named; do not exclude unilaterally.
- **No breach** — retrieved material mentions the ticker but does not describe
  the excluded activity. Do not flag. Retrieval relevance is not evidence of a
  violation.

## Ground every finding in a document

A finding without a verbatim supporting excerpt from a retrieved note is a
hallucination, regardless of how plausible it sounds. If the corpus does not
support a concern, the correct output is no finding — not a finding sourced to
general knowledge.

## Sector classification is not an exclusion

GICS sectors and exclusion categories are different things. A holding can sit
outside the Energy sector and still be a fossil-fuel exposure, or sit inside
Financials while being an excluded activity under some other screen. Check the
activity, not the label.

## When the mandate is under-specified

Weapons and coal screens in particular come in several constructions with
materially different reach. Where the mandate does not say which it means, name
the ambiguity in the finding and state which construction would and would not
capture the holding. Silently choosing one is worse than surfacing that a
choice exists.
