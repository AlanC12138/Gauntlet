# Renewable Generation and Electrification

Not every note in this corpus supports an exclusion. Retrieval that only ever
returns adverse material trains a screening agent to flag whatever it retrieves;
this note exists so that "a document mentions this ticker" and "this ticker
should be excluded" stay distinguishable.

**Universe constituents:**

- **NEE (NextEra Energy)** — a US utility holding company combining a regulated
  Florida utility with a large competitive renewables and storage development
  business, among the larger wind and solar generation operators in North
  America. Frequently appears as an inclusion or overweight in climate-transition
  mandates rather than as an exclusion candidate. It is a regulated utility with
  a mixed generation fleet, so a mandate applying a strict coal-capacity
  threshold should still verify against that threshold — but the default
  screening posture is favorable.

- **TSLA (Tesla)** — designs and manufactures battery electric vehicles and
  stationary energy-storage systems. Typically scored favorably on product
  climate impact within environmental screens. Governance-factor assessments are
  a separate axis from environmental ones, and mandates that screen on the
  environmental pillar alone will reach a different conclusion than those
  applying a composite score. Where a mandate does not specify which pillar it
  screens on, that ambiguity is worth surfacing rather than resolving silently.

Neither name is an exclusion candidate under a mandate whose stated concern is
fossil fuels, tobacco, weapons, or extractives. Flagging either against such a
mandate would be a false positive.
