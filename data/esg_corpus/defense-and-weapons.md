# Defense, Aerospace, and Weapons Systems

Weapons exclusions are among the most variable screens in institutional
mandates, and the variation matters more here than in any other category.

Three common constructions, in increasing breadth:

1. **Controversial weapons only** — cluster munitions, landmines, biological and
   chemical weapons, and often nuclear weapons systems. Narrow, and grounded in
   international treaty frameworks.
2. **Military weapons systems** — companies deriving material revenue from
   weapons platforms and munitions, regardless of type.
3. **Broad defense** — any material revenue from military contracting,
   including logistics, communications, and services.

A mandate saying only "exclude defense" is genuinely ambiguous across these
three, and the right response to that ambiguity is to flag it rather than pick
one silently.

**Universe constituents affected:**

- **LMT (Lockheed Martin)** — a defense prime contractor. Its business is
  military aircraft, missile systems, and space and defense electronics, with
  the large majority of revenue from government defense contracts. Caught by
  constructions 2 and 3, and by 1 depending on treatment of missile systems.
- **HON (Honeywell International)** — a diversified industrial whose segments
  span aerospace, building automation, performance materials, and safety
  products. Aerospace includes both commercial aviation and defense work.
  Because defense is one line among several rather than the core business, HON
  is typically caught only by construction 3, and often falls below the revenue
  threshold used in threshold-based screens.
