# Metals and Mining

Mining screens are motivated by land use, water consumption, tailings-management
risk, and community and indigenous-rights impacts rather than by the product
itself. Because the product is not inherently harmful in the way tobacco is,
categorical exclusions are rarer here; most mandates apply enhanced scrutiny,
engagement expectations, or norms-based screening (assessing conduct against
frameworks such as the UN Global Compact) instead of a blanket ban.

That makes mining a category where a screening agent should usually surface the
exposure for review rather than recommend exclusion outright, unless the mandate
explicitly names extractives.

**Universe constituents affected:**

- **NEM (Newmont)** — a gold mining company operating open-pit and underground
  mines across multiple jurisdictions. Directly in scope for any extractives
  screen.
- **GLD (SPDR Gold Shares)** — holds physical gold bullion in trust. It does
  **not** hold mining equities, so it carries no operational mining exposure and
  no corporate-conduct risk. It is included in this note specifically because
  the association is easy to make in error: a screen targeting mining companies
  does not reach a physically-backed bullion trust. Flagging GLD as a mining
  exposure would be a false positive.
